# Content Format Spec — Metafi V2.5

## Supported Formats

Dropdown values: `Auto`, `POV`, `Hot Take`, `Pain Mirror`, `Micro Story`, `Educational`

When `Auto` is selected, the planning phase chooses the most appropriate format based on the raw input.

---

## POV — 2 Slides

**Definition:** Places the viewer inside a specific moment or experience from a first-person lens.

**Slide count:** 2

**Format job:** Make the viewer feel like they are in the moment, not reading about it.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 (Hook): The moment — drop the viewer straight in, no setup
- Slide 2 (Close): The meaning — what that moment reveals or leaves behind

**When to use:** Raw input describes a real lived experience with a clear emotional weight.

**Avoid:** Explaining the moment instead of showing it. Generic "I felt" language. Moral conclusions.

**Example:**
> S1: You're mid-set. Bar on your back. And you realise you've been training alone for 14 months.
> S2: Some people just never show up. And that stopped hurting a long time ago.

---

## Hot Take — 1 Slide

**Definition:** One strong, debatable belief stated plainly and without apology.

**Slide count:** 1

**Format job:** Express a single conviction so clearly it forces a reaction — agree or disagree.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 (Hook): The take — no build-up, no softening, no explanation

**When to use:** Raw input contains a strong opinion, a contrarian view, or a belief most people hold quietly.

**Avoid:** Hedging. Adding context that waters down the statement. Two ideas competing on one slide.

**Example:**
> Rest days aren't lazy. Training every day is just fear wearing a gym bag.

---

## Pain Mirror — 3 Slides

**Definition:** Reflects a hidden or unspoken frustration back at the viewer with precision.

**Slide count:** 3

**Format job:** Make the viewer feel seen — not lectured to, not sold to.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 (Hook): Name the frustration — sharp, specific, no softening
- Slide 2 (Body): Go deeper — the layer underneath the frustration most people don't say out loud
- Slide 3 (Close): Land it — the real truth or shift that reframes the pain without fake positivity

**When to use:** Raw input captures a recurring struggle, a quiet resentment, or a feeling people recognise but rarely say.

**Avoid:** Offering solutions. Toxic positivity. Vague pain that could apply to anyone.

**Example:**
> S1: You train. You stay consistent. And nothing is visibly changing.
> S2: The problem isn't your effort. It's that no one told you what to actually look for.
> S3: Progress in the gym is almost always invisible until it suddenly isn't.

---

## Micro Story — 4 Slides

**Definition:** Shows a small, real sequence with friction — a moment that moved from one state to another.

**Slide count:** 4

**Format job:** Walk the viewer through a tight narrative arc that feels honest and earned.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 (Hook): The starting condition — set the scene fast
- Slide 2 (Body): The friction — something real gets in the way or shifts
- Slide 3 (Body): The turn — how it actually resolved or changed
- Slide 4 (Close): The takeaway — what it meant, without over-explaining

**When to use:** Raw input has a beginning, a tension point, and a real resolution — however small.

**Avoid:** Fake redemption arcs. Clean endings that erase the friction. Turning it into a motivational speech.

**Example:**
> S1: I trained for 6 months without tracking a single lift.
> S2: Felt like I was working hard. Couldn't prove it.
> S3: First week of logging — realised my "heavy" sets had barely moved.
> S4: The data wasn't discouraging. It was the first honest conversation I'd had with my training.

---

## Educational — 4 Slides

**Definition:** Teaches the raw input topic clearly, directly, and without filler.

**Slide count:** 4

**Format job:** Leave the viewer knowing something useful they didn't before — no fluff, no padding.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 (Hook): The question or gap — why this matters or what most people get wrong
- Slide 2 (Body): The core concept — the main thing to understand
- Slide 3 (Close): The application — what to actually do or think differently
- Slide 4 (Body): The reinforcement — a sharp follow-up point, stat, or nuance that locks it in

**When to use:** Raw input contains a clear teachable idea, a common misconception, or a practical method.

**Avoid:** Over-explaining. Lists of 7+ things. Advice so broad it could apply to anything.

**Example:**
> S1: Most people optimise the wrong variable when they plateau.
> S2: Volume isn't the issue. Intensity management is.
> S3: Before adding sets, audit your effort level on existing ones.
> S4: A hard set done right beats three moderate sets done out of habit.
